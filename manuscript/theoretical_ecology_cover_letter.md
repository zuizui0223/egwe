# Cover letter — Theoretical Ecology

Dear Editors,

Please consider our Article, **“Fragmentation separates biological states and local coupling shapes functional fate,”** for publication as a regular research article in *Theoretical Ecology*.

Functional extinction before numerical extinction, interaction rewiring, and false positives from early-warning signals are already established ecological concerns. Our question is different: **when conventional ecological and genetic summaries look identical, what local organization and life-cycle processes make functional futures diverge?** We connect a fragmentation experiment, an exact state-representation counterexample, prospectively locked causal interventions, and a full-denominator forecasting audit. Existing spatial moment-closure theory, genetic-load metapopulation models, interaction rewiring-potential research and network-node warning theory have established important parts of the background (Murrell et al. 2004; Olusanya et al. 2025; Marjakangas et al. 2025; Masuda et al. 2024). Our advance is not the general observation that space, feedback, potential interactions or sentinel selection matters. It is a single model-conditional evidence chain that starts from *identical ecological/genetic marginals*, identifies the operators that sort or repair hidden spatial mismatches, and tests whether their remaining local joint-state reserve adds information beyond the co-timed local-interaction maximum.

First, in a fixed-area fragmentation gradient, potential high-trait viability disappeared under every tested subdivision of 1,037 supported source states, while realised trait occupancy remained approximately 99.6–100% at the finite observation horizon. Interaction strength, local effective population size and realised high-trait mass also followed different trajectories. These are closure-specific demonstrations of state separation, not claims that fragmentation must generate the same pattern in nature.

Second, two constructed systems with the same conventional census, interaction, trait and allele-frequency marginals—and the same diversity and differentiation indices—differed in exact next-generation interaction state by as much as **0.2543**. Their cross-layer covariance altered local support variance **49-fold**. We then moved beyond that mathematical possibility to isolate processes. A prospective 6,000-pair intervention attributed **+6.883 percentage points** of the generation-40 arrangement contrast (95% CI +5.800 to +7.967) to local interaction-dependent allele sorting. Exact recruitment and feedback identities identify countervailing mismatch buffering and recoupling; a separate density-edge deletion identifies a failure-amplification gate. Notably, an independently locked route-duration experiment did *not* support the simple expectation that recoupling prolongs time above the viability switch.

Third, the distinction between early change and fate information changes the monitoring interpretation. Six genetic-diversity thresholds preceded all observed losses but fired in every non-event, giving specificity zero and binary AUC 0.5. A separately preregistered 12,000-trajectory holdout instead found that a **continuous strongest-local reserve** ranked later functional loss with AUC **0.92734**, adding **+0.02135** AUC (95% CI +0.01770 to +0.02501) beyond the co-timed strongest interaction state. This is an incremental, model-conditional ranking result: the holdout used no migration and two fixed initial configurations. The first natural prospective transfer did not establish additional predictive information, so we do not claim that the predictor or numerical threshold has been validated in the wild.

We submit this as an **operator-resolved theoretical-ecology explanation of state separation and functional fate**, rather than as a universal genetic warning rule or a claim that functional loss before extinction was newly discovered. The finite results are tied to exact equations, frozen protocols and reproducible code; the natural evidence is explicitly bounded. No substantially overlapping standalone manuscript is being simultaneously submitted while this flagship is under consideration.

Author names, contributions, funding, competing interests and any required automated-assistance disclosure are subject to corresponding-author verification in the final submission metadata.

We believe the manuscript fits *Theoretical Ecology* because it uses analytic results, an explicitly finite eco-genetic simulation, and prospectively frozen interventions to answer an ecological question: how fragmentation changes functional persistence even when simple abundance and diversity summaries agree.
