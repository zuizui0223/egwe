## Central question

> **What must be preserved, observed, and validated before ecological deterioration is forecastable?**

## Candidate abstract

Ecological prediction can fail before a forecasting model is ever fitted. A disturbance may generate several biologically distinct states; an analytical summary may erase structure that changes the future; an early marker may precede every observed event while failing to distinguish events from non-events; and a natural proxy may lose its intended information before residual environmental context is interpreted. We combine exact results, prospectively locked finite-model experiments, a full-denominator warning audit, and seven locked natural-data analyses to separate these failure points. Under fragmentation, potential viability, realised occupancy, interaction support, effective size and realised trait mass did not behave as one deterioration axis. States with identical common ecological and genetic marginals but opposite cross-layer alignment differed in their exact next transition by 0.2543, and a separately locked 1,500-pair experiment showed an approximately five-percentage-point excess functional-loss risk at generations 20 and 40 under the anti-aligned state. Conversely, six frozen diversity thresholds preceded every observed loss in two ensembles yet fired in every non-event, yielding specificity zero and binary AUC 0.5. In natural data, endpoint-relevant measurement, information-preserving representation, residual-context testing and cross-study identifiability produced distinct outcomes rather than one common ecological effect. Together these results support a predictive-validity hierarchy: ecological forecasts require explicit biological state, target-relevant representation, full-denominator validation and empirically adequate measurement. Success at one layer cannot substitute for validation at the next.

## 1. Introduction — prediction can fail before modelling

Ecological forecasting is often framed as a problem of choosing an algorithm, estimating parameters and quantifying uncertainty. A logically prior problem is whether the object supplied to the forecast is itself valid. Four distinct collapses can occur:

1. multiple biological states can be treated as one disturbance response;
2. a low-dimensional representation can erase target-relevant organization;
3. temporal precedence can be mistaken for predictive discrimination;
4. a natural measurement can be credited as a state coordinate before it has earned endpoint relevance or preserved its mechanistic information.

The central argument is not that one universal ecological state exists. It is that prediction requires a validated chain from biological state to representation, warning and measurement.

We express that chain as

`X_t -> phi(X_t) -> g(phi(X_0:t)) -> Y_(t+h)`,

with an additional empirical measurement map `M_t -> estimated state` in natural systems. Each arrow is an inferential obligation rather than a default identity.

## 2. A four-layer predictive-validity hierarchy

### Layer 1 — biological-state distinction

Question: does one upstream disturbance determine one biological deterioration state?

Evidence source: EGC.

The parent theory and finite fragmentation gradient distinguish interaction support, potential high-trait viability, realised high-trait occupancy, local effective size, realised high-trait mass, allele persistence and genetic diversity. In the fresh fixed-area gradient, potential viability changed from 1,037/1,037 at one patch to 0/1,037 at every tested subdivision, while realised occupancy persisted at approximately 99.6–100% at generation 30. Interaction and local effective size continued to decline with patch count, whereas realised high-trait mass partially recovered after its initial loss.

Inference: a disturbance label cannot substitute for the biological state relevant to a prediction target.

Boundary: this result does not establish which compressed state representation is forecast-sufficient.

### Layer 2 — representation adequacy

Question: can matching standard ecological and genetic summaries still hide different futures?

Evidence source: EGWE state-validity.

Aligned and anti-aligned states match census, interaction, allele-frequency and trait marginals, H_alpha, H_gamma and F_ST, while reversing patchwise cross-layer alignment. Their exact next interaction transition differs by as much as 0.2543. In a separately prospectively locked 1,500-pair propagation experiment under one fixed deterioration path, anti-aligned minus aligned functional-loss risk was 0.0 percentage points at generation 5, +0.33 at 10, +5.33 at 20 and +5.20 at 40.

Inference: representation adequacy is target-, horizon- and operator-relative; matching marginals do not establish dynamic equivalence.

Boundary: the aligned/anti-aligned contrast is not claimed to be the cause of warning failure.

### Layer 3 — warning validity

Question: does repeated temporal precedence establish predictive warning performance?

Evidence source: EGWE warning-validity.

Six frozen H_alpha/H_gamma relative-diversity thresholds preceded all observed functional-loss events in both the inherited and independently seeded fresh ensembles (35/35 and 33/33). The same thresholds also fired in all horizon non-events (48/48 and 49/49), giving sensitivity 1, specificity 0 and binary-marker AUC 0.5 for every rule in both ensembles. An exact denominator result shows that perfect event-conditioned precedence leaves non-event specificity unidentified.

Inference: temporal ordering among events and prospective discrimination on the full denominator are different statistical objects.

Boundary: this rejects the six frozen rules in the tested state; it does not show that genetic diversity contains no predictive information generally.

### Layer 4 — empirical measurement and identifiability

Question: when does a natural measurement earn the right to represent a state before residual context is interpreted?

Evidence source: EGWEE.

Seven locked natural-data analyses occupy distinct branches of a four-gate sequence: endpoint-relevant measurement adequacy, information-preserving representation, transferable residual-context testing and cross-study identifiability. Honshu–Izu, Zurich and Toronto showed no detected transferable residual-context gain after their locked partial states; Oenothera identified a missing contemporary spatial mating coordinate; Eschscholzia and Mallorca carob did not earn endpoint-relevant adequacy for their candidate pollinator proxies; Campanula showed representation collapse after feature-wise standardisation; and the cross-origin synthesis remained non-identifiable.

Inference: a plausible biological proxy, a richer raw measurement or a negative residual does not automatically license a stronger ecological interpretation.

Boundary: these natural systems do not validate the synthetic EGC/EGWE closure and are not pooled into one ecological effect.

## 3. Four constructive non-implications

The unified evidence supports four explicit non-implications:

1. `common disturbance != one biological state`;
2. `matching marginals != matching futures`;
3. `perfect precedence != predictive discrimination`;
4. `plausible proxy != validated empirical state`.

The paper's positive claim is the hierarchy created by these non-implications: downstream predictive interpretation requires validation at each layer.

## 4. Why these layers are dependent without being causal substitutes

The layers are ordered by inference, not by demonstrated causal mediation.

- EGC establishes that state objects can separate; it does not prove the EGWE coarse representation is the unique correct state.
- EGWE-state shows that a particular hidden cross-layer structure matters for one transition and one deterioration path; it does not explain the diversity-threshold false positives.
- EGWE-warning identifies a denominator failure; it does not require the state-validity result to hold.
- EGWEE asks whether candidate natural measurements deserve state status; it does not provide external validation of the finite simulator.

This separation is essential. The hierarchy says **what must be checked next**, not that one observed failure mechanically caused the next.

## 5. A decisive end-to-end natural test

The current evidence is sufficient for a hierarchy/counterexample paper but not for a claim that one measured natural joint state is end-to-end forecast-sufficient. A decisive future test would synchronize, within one natural system and before outcome inspection:

- upstream disturbance/context;
- local demographic/resource support;
- realised interaction state;
- partner/mating/pollen connectivity;
- target-relevant trait/genetic coordinates and cross-layer alignment;
- a declared future functional or reproductive endpoint;
- event and non-event units through a shared horizon;
- an independently frozen validation cohort or repeated future windows.

The primary nested held-out comparison would be:

`context-only -> coarse marginals -> joint/process state -> warning -> joint state + warning -> + origin/history`.

This design could directly test whether state representation improves forecasting, whether the candidate warning adds discrimination after the state is known, and whether origin/history still contributes after the measured state has earned endpoint relevance.

## 6. Discussion — prediction is a validation stack

The four evidence layers suggest a general discipline for ecological forecasting. A forecast target is not made valid by a biologically plausible disturbance label, a familiar diversity statistic, an impressive lead time or a mechanistically appealing proxy. Predictive validity depends on a chain of explicit mappings. Biological states must first be distinguished; analytical representations must preserve target-relevant information; candidate warnings must be tested on the full denominator; and natural measurements must earn endpoint relevance before residual context is interpreted.

This hierarchy complements ecological forecast-horizon and iterative-forecasting frameworks by locating failure modes upstream of forecast algorithm choice. It also provides a way to treat negative and STOP results constructively: a failed gate identifies which inference cannot yet be made and what measurement or design would be needed next.

## 7. Claim boundary

Supported:

- the four non-implications above are each established by their owning locked evidence;
- they can be ordered as distinct obligations in one predictive-validity hierarchy;
- success at one layer does not logically license a downstream claim without additional validation.

Not supported:

- one universal eco-genetic state vector;
- a claim that fragmentation caused the aligned/anti-aligned state contrast;
- a claim that omitted alignment caused warning failure;
- a universal generation-20 timescale;
- external validation of the synthetic closure by the seven natural systems;
- one pooled urban/island/pollination effect;
- full end-to-end natural forecast sufficiency from the present archives.
