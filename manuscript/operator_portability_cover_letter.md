Dear Editors of *Ecological Modelling*,

Please consider our Short Communication, **“Connectivity is an operator, not a scalar: a finite-model portability test.”**

The manuscript addresses a modelling-identification problem rather than proposing a new generic connectivity metric. A stochastic pattern originally associated with one allele-frequency-mixing parameter is challenged in two distinct ways: first by a prospectively locked replication on an independent seed ensemble, and second by replacing the biological movement operator while retaining the same historical reference ensemble. The historical pattern did not reproduce on fresh seeds and did not appear under either whole-individual dispersal or paternal-gamete pollen flow.

The contribution is deliberately narrower than the established observation that connectivity has multiple biological meanings. We instead provide a controlled portability audit showing why a numerical connectivity label is insufficient to transfer a stochastic interpretation across movement operators without an identification or calibration argument. We also report the evidence structure explicitly: the two operator-substitution experiments reuse the same historical no-connectivity and allele-only reference observations and are therefore not counted as independent baseline replications.

Because the key operator-specific results are negative, we quantified the resolution of the completed design without opening new simulations. The five-block equal-rate tests had an 80% power benchmark near Cohen-style Pearson homogeneity effect size `w=0.163`, corresponding to roughly 8.1–8.2 percentage points weighted RMS block-rate deviation near a pooled loss rate of 0.5. We therefore frame the result as a precision-bounded portability failure rather than equivalence.
