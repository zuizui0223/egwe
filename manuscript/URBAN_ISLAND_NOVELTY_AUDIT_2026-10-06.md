# Urban–island novelty audit — compensation architecture, not fragmentation labels

## Decision

The cross-system novelty should **not** be framed as any of the following:

- fragmentation can reduce ecological function before population disappearance;
- ecological interactions matter beyond species richness;
- functional diversity or trait matching matters;
- urban systems are not simple genetic islands;
- islands are filtered by mutualist availability;
- network structure can affect persistence;
- movement or rewiring can sometimes compensate disturbance.

All of those statements already have strong external precedents.

The sharper contribution is:

> **the same upstream loss of local support can lead to opposite functional futures because biological processes can either propagate mismatch or open compensatory routes; therefore functional fate is determined by compensation architecture rather than by fragmentation geometry or origin label alone.**

## What is already known

### Functional loss before numerical loss

Functional extinction can precede the disappearance of the focal species itself. This is an established community-ecology result and cannot serve as the flagship novelty.

### Persistence capacity beyond richness

Recent island-network work goes further: reduced island area can lower the persistence capacity of mutualistic assemblages beyond its effect on species richness. Thus “future viability/persistence contains information beyond present richness” is also no longer sufficient as a novelty claim.

### Functional matching beyond species counts

Island/mainland pollination work shows that pollinator functional diversity and trait matching can predict pollination function better than pollinator species diversity alone.

### Urban connectivity is not simple isolation

Recent urban plant-genetics synthesis reports substantial genetic diversity and gene flow despite spatial fragmentation. Urban geometry therefore cannot be translated directly into genetic isolation.

### Mutualist filters on islands

Oceanic-island richness is shaped not only by area and isolation but also by the availability/co-colonisation of mutualists.

## What the present programme adds

### 1. A constructive state-sufficiency failure

The matched-marginal construction holds conventional ecological and genetic marginals fixed while changing only cross-layer spatial association. The exact next interaction field changes by as much as 0.2543. This does more than show that “interactions matter”: it demonstrates that a declared marginal state representation is not transition-sufficient.

### 2. Opposing operators, not one monotone fragmentation effect

Prospective intervention decomposes later fate into processes with opposing effects:

- q-dependent allele selection **sorts** compatible eco-genetic states and can amplify spatial differentiation;
- recruitment **buffers** trait–allele mismatch;
- direct feedback **recouples** ecological interaction state toward the local trait/genetic bundle;
- density feedback opens a **failure/amplification gate** once headroom is consumed.

The novelty is not the existence of any one process. It is that the sign of the future response depends on their balance.

### 3. A natural inversion pair already exists

Two established natural study programmes show why this architecture matters.

**Urban Montpellier — *Crepis sancta*.**  
Low local flowering density reduced pollinator activity and seed set, while separate parentage work in the same metapopulation documented nonzero wider pollen/seed movement. This is an **uncompensated local interaction-loss route despite movement**.

**Miyake-jima — *Camellia japonica*–*Zosterops japonicus*.**  
Volcanic disturbance reduced local floral resources, but pollinator movement broadened, pollen immigration/donor mixing increased, and pollination plus next-generation mixing were maintained. This is a **movement-compensated route despite local resource loss**.

These are not synchronized matched experiments and cannot estimate an urban-versus-island effect. Their role is mechanistic: the sign of functional response is not fixed by geography or by the amount of local resource loss.

### 4. The unresolved urban–island test is now specific

The direct cross-origin convergence question has already been prospectively audited in EGWEE and remains not identifiable from existing heterogeneous archives because state semantics, response definitions and origin/study identity are confounded.

The next valid test is therefore not “urban versus island” in general.

It is:

> **after measuring the same future-relevant state and compensatory processes, does origin/history still improve prediction of subsequent function?**

Operationally:

`M0: F_future ~ joint_state + compensation_coordinates`

versus

`M1: F_future ~ joint_state + compensation_coordinates + origin/history`.

If origin/history adds no held-out information, distinct fragmentation routes have converged on the same functional regime. If it does, the state is missing a process or memory coordinate.

## Why this is less self-evident

“Spatial arrangement matters” is expected.

The nontrivial prediction is instead a **sign reversal conditional on process architecture**:

> local support loss does not imply functional decline if compensatory movement/recoupling outruns the loss process, while apparent connectivity does not guarantee function if movement does not restore the local interaction bottleneck.

This predicts that two landscapes with the same amount of habitat loss, local density decline, occupancy or even broad connectivity can move in opposite functional directions.

## Manuscript-level consequence

The flagship should therefore avoid making urban–island convergence a present result. The natural systems are used as bounded mechanistic anchors:

- *Crepis*: movement without local functional rescue;
- Miyake *Camellia*: movement-mediated compensation.

The finite model contributes the experimentally resolved operator logic that can generate those opposite classes of future.

The broader claim is:

> **Fragmentation does not have a fixed functional sign once local support is lost; fate depends on whether the remaining biological processes propagate incompatibility or compensate it.**
