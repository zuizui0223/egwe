Dear Editors,

Please consider our manuscript, **“Matching eco-genetic summaries can hide different ecological futures,”** as a Letter in *Ecology Letters*.

Ecological forecasting usually begins with a description of the present state and asks what happens next. Our study addresses a prior question: **when does that state description preserve the distinctions that actually matter for the prediction target and forecast horizon?** We use a finite eco-genetic system to construct two present states that are identical in census, interaction, allele-frequency and realised-trait marginals, `H_alpha`, `H_gamma`, and `F_ST`, while differing only in patchwise cross-layer alignment. Their exact next interaction transitions differ by as much as **0.2543**. Matching standard ecological and genetic summaries is therefore insufficient to establish dynamic equivalence under the declared closure.

We then tested whether this hidden state distinction propagates beyond the next transition. The original frozen 500-pair, 60-generation campaign estimated an anti-aligned minus aligned loss-risk difference of **+4.4 percentage points**, but with a paired 95% interval from **-1.2 to +10.0 points**. Rather than treating that non-significant contrast as evidence of no effect, we opened a separate propagation experiment under a protocol locked before its outcomes were inspected. The experiment retained one common deterioration path, predeclared readouts at generations 5, 10, 20 and 40, and nested paired samples of 500, 1,000 and 1,500 trajectories.

In the primary 1,500-pair curve, the loss-risk contrast was **0.0 points at generation 5**, **+0.33 points at generation 10** (95% CI -0.44 to +1.11), **+5.33 points at generation 20** (+2.04 to +8.62), and **+5.20 points at generation 40** (+1.96 to +8.44). The nested replication prefixes showed that increasing paired replication primarily narrowed uncertainty around the generation-20/40 effect-size estimates rather than creating a qualitatively new contrast. Under this declared forcing path, a cross-layer state distinction erased by standard marginals therefore became visible as a roughly five-percentage-point functional-loss risk difference between the 10- and 20-generation readouts and remained similar through generation 40.

A second result sets the portability boundary. A historical pattern associated with an allele-frequency mixing operator did not reproduce in an independently seeded ensemble and did not transfer to separately declared whole-individual or pollen-only movement closures. We therefore treat process state as operator-specific unless an identification argument shows that different operators preserve the same target-relevant information.

We believe this study fits *Ecology Letters* because its contribution is a general ecological forecasting principle rather than a parameter sensitivity result: **state representation adequacy is relative to the prediction target, forecast horizon and biological operator**. The constructive counterexample establishes the representation problem exactly within the declared model, while the prospectively locked propagation experiment shows that the hidden distinction can acquire a measurable endpoint consequence over time. The manuscript is deliberately bounded: it does not claim that generation 20 is a universal biological cutoff, that the finite state is complete, or that the same timescale applies in nature.

The state-validity manuscript is independent of our separately routed warning-validity and natural-data manuscripts. In particular, we do not claim that cross-layer alignment explains or repairs the frozen genetic-warning rules, and no natural dataset is used as validation of the synthetic closure. This separation keeps the present manuscript focused on the representation and forecast-horizon problem supported by its own evidence.
